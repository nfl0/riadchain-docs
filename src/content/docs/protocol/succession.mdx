---
title: Succession consensus
description: Follow automatic funded-account production, timed successor rotation and verified operator recovery.
---

Riadchain uses succession-based proof of stake for private home and company networks. One trusted signing account produces blocks at a time. If it stops, funded successors receive deterministic timeout windows to resume production.

This assumes trusted operators and protected keys. It does not provide Byzantine quorum finality or automatic resolution of network partitions.

## Producer and followers

Native nodes verify and replay history automatically. An owner below the minimum confirmed spendable balance remains ineligible. Funding it to the minimum makes it an eligible standby; its owner key or authorized operational signer produces when local readiness permits.

There is no validator registry, fixed runtime role or mining flag. The current producer also needs the minimum balance. Unclaimed rewards do not confer eligibility.

Keep distinct keys on machines in separate storage failure domains. A peer invitation shares public bootstrap information; permission to exchange peer traffic remains separate from funded production eligibility.

## Owner and machine keys

A funded owner can authorize one operational machine key in signed genesis or through an owner-signed transaction. Blocks identify the owner; the machine signs without inheriting owner spending, reward-claim or contract ownership authority. Rewards and succession eligibility remain with the owner. P2P traffic authenticates the machine separately.

Owner-signed `setSigner` or `revokeSigner` calls change the binding for the next block. A new key cannot sign the block that first authorizes it. Local [`production_owner` configuration](/node/configuration/) selects the owner without granting authority. Revoked machines remain archive followers.

The owner always retains direct signing for deliberate recovery if the operational key is lost. Restore verified history and FILEs and fence old producers first; do not run two producers for one owner. Keep a separately funded rescue owner. Limited machine keys protect account authority, while a compromised machine can still censor or disrupt production and damage its local data. Guided phone provisioning remains in development.

## Choose the successor deterministically

Selection uses authoritative state before the proposed block:

1. Find account addresses other than the current producer whose aggregate spendable UTXO balance meets `minStakeSatoshis`.
2. Order addresses after the current producer, wrapping to the lowest address.
3. Give each successor a timeout-sized window, rotating again after the list is exhausted.
4. For the selected address, use its lowest ordered `(transaction ID, output index)` active UTXO as the proof.

The proof identifies that exact output and amount. Its individual amount can be below the minimum when the account's aggregate balance qualifies. It cannot be spent in the succession block.

Offline funded wallets do not permanently block a later ready successor: the window advances. Funding a standby must happen while blocks are still being produced; an unconfirmed transfer cannot restart a stalled chain.

## Validate chain time

An ordinary block must be at least one block interval after its parent. A succession block must be at least one succession timeout after its parent, and its producer must own the selected window at that timestamp.

Downtime can be arbitrarily long; historical validation imposes no upper gap limit. The funded current producer may resume an ordinary block after a long outage. Time alone does not authorize arbitrary accounts to produce.

These rules use chain timestamps and checked integer arithmetic. Live admission separately rejects blocks more than two seconds ahead of the receiving clock. Keep clocks synchronized.

## Validate every block

Nodes verify the authorized signature, canonical hashes and parent linkage, timing, succession proof, execution limits, FILE commitments and resulting state hash. Invalid candidates cannot change canonical state.

A configured node ordinarily needs successful authenticated synchronization before taking over. When peers are unavailable after a cold restart, an operator can use verified [isolated recovery](/node/backup-recovery/#resume-production-with-unavailable-peers). This is an explicit recovery decision, not evidence that an unreachable peer has stopped.

## Fail-stop on a validated fork

A fully validated same-parent conflict records durable evidence in `fork-alarm.json` and stops mutation and production. Restarting or isolated recovery does not clear the alarm or select a branch.

Preserve evidence, compare independently verified history and coordinate recovery outside the node. A lost key can be replaced by a surviving funded recovery identity; a seed phrase does not recreate chain history or FILE bytes. [Recovery](/node/backup-recovery/) explains these boundaries.
