---
title: Succession consensus
description: Understand trusted block production, timed takeover and conflict handling on a private network.
---

Private Riadchain networks use trusted succession. A single eligible owner produces ordinary blocks. If that producer stops, other eligible owners receive deterministic takeover windows. [Proof of stake](/protocol/proof-of-stake/) describes the separate public-consensus design.

## Producer and followers

Production eligibility follows aggregate confirmed spendable RDC and the applicable signing authority. The private setup defaults to **10,000 RDC** and a **one-hour succession timeout**. The signed genesis binds the actual values. Unclaimed rewards do not count toward the balance.

An enrolled machine needs zero RDC to join over its authenticated peer routes, verify history and serve FILEs. Funding its signing account lets it participate in succession once the transfer confirms and the machine is synchronized. The node has no permanent producer or follower role.

## Owner and machine keys

A funded owner can authorize an operational signer. Blocks identify the owner and verify the machine's authorized ML-DSA-65 signature. That machine key does not inherit owner spending or reward-claim authority. The Android setup keeps the owner keys in the wallet and gives the appliance its own operational credentials. Native CLI setup can instead prepare directly signing node identities, whose private seeds also authorize spending for those accounts.

## Choose the successor deterministically

Takeover candidates follow account-address order after the incumbent, wrapping to the beginning. Candidates receive timeout-sized windows. If the first eligible candidate is offline, its window expires and the next candidate's opens. A candidate can publish a provisional succession block only after its authorized window opens.

After an authenticated startup sync and an incumbent outage, a qualifying node can establish ordinary production on the next block cadence following its provisional claim. New transactions can confirm while the former producer remains offline. Existing producers also continue normal production when a follower is offline.

## Verify recovery and resolve conflicts

A returning node on the same history prefix can synchronize to the new producer. At the first divergence, a valid incumbent block produced before the takeover window takes priority while the successor's entire branch remains provisional.

If both conflicting branches contain ordinary blocks, the node preserves their signed history and stops production. Authenticated reads remain available for comparison. Keep both history logs and FILE archives and coordinate recovery outside the node. See [back up and recover](/node/backup-recovery/).
